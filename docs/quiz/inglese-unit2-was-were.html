Inglese · Unit 2

Was, were, had e descrivere le persone

2ª media · un percorso in 15 passi: il passato di be (affermativo, negativo, domande), had, le espressioni di tempo, le parole per descrivere aspetto e carattere, un testo da leggere e due da scrivere. Ogni passo si sblocca scrivendo, riordinando o smistando: niente da indovinare. L'ultimo passo è una verifica simulata.

🔒
1

Was e were: il passato di beI was · you were · he was · we were

Per dire com’era qualcosa o dov’era qualcuno ieri, l’inglese usa il Past simple di be: due sole forme, was e were.

was

I was
he was · she was · it was
e ogni nome singolare: Mark was, the film was

were

you were
we were · they were
e ogni nome plurale: the boys were, Tina and Mia were

Attenzione a you: in inglese «tu eri» e «voi eravate» si dicono allo stesso modo, you were. Mai you was.

Quando si usa. Per azioni e situazioni iniziate e finite nel passato. In italiano corrisponde a tre tempi: era (imperfetto), è stato (passato prossimo), fu (passato remoto). The workshop was brilliant = il laboratorio era / è stato fantastico. We were in the library = eravamo in biblioteca.

Anche l’età si dice con be: I was thirteen in 2021 (avevo tredici anni). E «sono nato» è I was born: ne parliamo al passo 6.

🔒
2

Le espressioni di tempo al passatoyesterday · last night · two days ago · in 2012

Una frase al passato di solito dice anche quando. Le espressioni di tempo si costruiscono in quattro modi.

yesterday + momento del giorno

yesterday morning · yesterday afternoon · yesterday evening
ma «ieri sera / ieri notte» = last night, mai yesterday night

last + giorno, settimana, mese, stagione, anno

last night · last Monday · last week · last month · last summer · last year · last Christmas
senza the e senza in: «last week», non «in the last week»

periodo di tempo + ago

ten minutes ago · two days ago · a week ago · five years ago
= dieci minuti fa, due giorni fa: «ago» viene dopo

in + mese, anno, decennio

in June · in 2012 · in the 1960s
con l’anno niente articolo (in 2012); con il decennio sì (in the 1960s)

Ago, in, back. Two days ago = due giorni fa (passato). In two days = fra due giorni (futuro): stessa parola in, significato opposto! Nell’inglese americano parlato senti anche ten minutes back, che vuol dire la stessa cosa di ago; in verifica però usa sempre ago.

Dove va l’espressione di tempo? All’inizio o alla fine della frase, senza cambiare significato: Yesterday evening Tina was at a piano lesson = Tina was at a piano lesson yesterday evening.

Le stesse parole ti dicono anche quale tempo verbale usare: now, at the moment → presente; every day, always, on Mondays → abitudine; yesterday, last…, …ago, in 2012 → passato; tomorrow, next week → futuro.

🔒
3

La forma negativawasn’t · weren’t

Per dire che qualcosa non era così, basta aggiungere not dopo was / were. Quasi sempre si usa la forma contratta.

forma intera

I was not · he / she / it was not
you / we / they were not

forma contratta (la più usata)

I wasn’t · he / she / it wasn’t
you / we / they weren’t

Ruby wasn’t at the workshop yesterday = Ruby non era al laboratorio ieri. There weren’t a lot of people = non c’era molta gente. It wasn’t boring, but it wasn’t very cool = non era noioso, ma non era molto forte.

Nella verifica scrivi bene l’apostrofo: wasn’t, weren’t. Non esistono wasn’t not né didn’t was: con be al passato non serve mai did.

🔒
4

Le domande e le risposte breviWas he…? Yes, he was. · Were you…? No, I wasn’t.

Per fare una domanda non servono do o did: si mette il verbo prima del soggetto.

affermazione

The workshop was good.
You were at the cinema.
There were a lot of people.

domanda

Was the workshop good?
Were you at the cinema?
Were there a lot of people?

Le risposte brevi non ripetono tutta la frase: Yes, + pronome + was/were oppure No, + pronome + wasn’t/weren’t. Nel «no» si usa sempre la forma contratta.

DomandaSìNo
Was I late?Yes, you were.No, you weren’t.
Were you at home?Yes, I was. / Yes, we were.No, I wasn’t. / No, we weren’t.
Was he / she / it good?Yes, he / she / it was.No, he / she / it wasn’t.
Were we right?Yes, you were.No, you weren’t.
Were they there?Yes, they were.No, they weren’t.

Occhio allo scambio di persona: se ti chiedono Were you…? rispondi con I (o we); se chiedono Was I…? rispondi con you. E Mum and Dad, the boys, the tickets nella risposta diventano they.

🔒
5

Le domande con When, Where, Why, Who, What, HowWhere were you? · How old were you in 2016?

Per chiedere quando, dove, perché, chi, cosa, come si mette la parola interrogativa davanti e poi si continua come nella domanda sì/no: parola interrogativa + was/were + soggetto.

Question wordVerbSubject
Whenwereyouin France?
Whywasheearly?
Wherewasshelast night?
Whoweretheywith?
Whatwasthe nameof the film?
How oldwereyouin 2021?
Howwasthe workshop?

Significati: when = quando · where = dove · why = perché · who = chi · what = cosa, quale · how old = quanti anni · how = come (com’è andata).

Due domande da non confondere: How was the party? (com’è andata la festa?) e What was the weather like? (com’era il tempo?). Nella seconda like va alla fine, e vale anche per il cibo, le persone, la guida: What was the food like? What was your guide like?

🔒
6

Was born · There was / there wereI was born in 2012 · There was a fire in 1613

Due usi speciali di was/were che nella verifica compaiono quasi sempre.

Essere nati: was / were born

In inglese «sono nato» si dice con be: I was born in 2012. When were you born? (quando sei nato?) Where was your mum born? (dov’è nata tua mamma?) My grandparents were born in Sicily.

C’era, c’erano: there was / there were

Come there is / there are, ma al passato. Il verbo si accorda con ciò che viene dopo: There was a terrible fire (singolare) · There were a lot of people (plurale).

negativa

There wasn’t a roof.
There weren’t any women actors.

domanda

Was there a party? – Yes, there was. / No, there wasn’t.
Were there a lot of people? – No, there weren’t.

🔒
7

Had: il passato di haveI had · she had · they had (uguale per tutti)

Il passato di have e di have got è una parola sola per tutte le persone: had. Nella verifica serve solo la forma affermativa.

adesso

I have got a dog. · She has got long hair. · They’ve got a blue car.

nel passato

I had a cat. · She had short hair. · They had a red car.

Was o had? La trappola dell’italiano

In italiano diciamo «aveva 13 anni» e «aveva una casa» con lo stesso verbo. In inglese no:

  • was / were per com’era, dov’era, quanti anni aveva, da dove veniva: I was 13, Sam was quite short, we were at a concert, they were from Wales.
  • had per le cose possedute, i capelli, gli occhi, gli animali, le feste, i momenti: they had a house, Ruby had black hair, we had two cats, she had a big party, we had a great time (ci siamo divertiti).

Le espressioni di tempo decidono tra presente e passato: My dad is bald now, but he had long hair when he was 16.

🔒
8

Le parole dell’aspetto fisicotall, slim, curly hair, freckles, glasses…

Per descrivere una persona servono parole precise. Eccole raggruppate come nel libro: imparale a blocchi.

Age · età

young giovane · middle-aged di mezza età · old anziano

Height · altezza

tall alto · short basso

Weight · corporatura

slim magro, snello · overweight sovrappeso · average weight di peso medio

General appearance · aspetto generale

beautiful bella · handsome bello (di un uomo) · good-looking di bell’aspetto · smart elegante · trendy alla moda · well-dressed ben vestito · scruffy trasandato

Face · viso

forma: long · oval · round
peli: beard barba · moustache baffi

Eyes · occhi

blue · brown · green · dark scuri

Hair · capelli

lunghezza: short · shoulder-length fino alle spalle · long
tipo: straight lisci · wavy mossi · curly ricci · spiky a punta
colore: black · brown · blond · red rossi · grey grigi
stile: fringe frangia · ponytail coda · plaits trecce · Afro · dreadlocks

Other words · altri dettagli

bald calvo · freckles lentiggini · glasses occhiali · braces apparecchio ai denti · scar cicatrice · tattoo tatuaggio

L’ordine degli aggettivi dei capelli è lunghezza + colore + stile: She’s got long, blond, curly hair oppure Her hair is long, blond and curly. E ricorda: hair è singolare, mai hairs.

9

Descrivere una persona: le frasiHe’s tall. He’s got brown eyes. He wears glasses.

Tre verbi bastano per quasi tutte le descrizioni.

be → com’è

altezza, peso, età, bello/brutto, calvo
He’s tall and slim. · She’s young: she’s about 20. · He’s bald.

have got → cosa ha

occhi, capelli, barba, baffi, lentiggini, apparecchio
He’s got brown eyes. · She’s got shoulder-length, red, wavy hair. · He’s got a moustache.

wear → cosa indossa

occhiali, vestiti
He wears glasses. (va bene anche he’s got glasses)

i rafforzativi

very / really = molto, davvero: He’s very tall. She’s really slim.
quite = abbastanza: She’s quite short.
not very = non molto: He isn’t very young.
a bit = un po’ (per i difetti): He’s a bit bossy.

Una descrizione completa, come quelle del libro: Rebecca’s tall and average weight. She’s got shoulder-length, red, wavy hair and green eyes. And she’s got freckles! She’s young: she’s about 20 years old.

Per l’età approssimativa: he’s about 70 (ha circa 70 anni), she’s in her twenties. Per l’età esatta: she’s 13 years old o semplicemente she’s 13.

10

Il carattere: What’s he like?friendly · funny · shy · bossy · lazy · nice

Dopo l’aspetto, il carattere. Le parole del libro:

👍

nice simpatico, gentile · friendly amichevole · funny divertente · kind buono, gentile · clever intelligente

👉 👎

shy timido · quiet tranquillo · bossy prepotente · lazy pigro · silly sciocco · rude maleducato

Le due domande da non confondere

DomandaChiedeRisposta
What does he / she look like?l’aspetto fisicoHe’s tall and slim. / She’s got blond, curly hair.
What’s he / she like?il carattereHe’s funny. / She’s shy but really nice.

look like = assomigliare a, avere l’aspetto di → does + look like. be like = essere fatto così → is / ’s + like. In entrambe like va in fondo e non si traduce con «piacere».

Per presentare qualcuno: Who’s Hannah? – She’s my cousin. Poi: What’s she like? – She’s really nice. E infine: What does she look like? – She’s quite tall, and she’s got…

11

Due dialoghi: il nuovo amico e la foto delle vacanzeWhere were you last night? · Who were you with?

Nella verifica le domande arrivano spesso dentro un dialogo. Ecco i due tipi dell’unità.

1. Parlare di una persona nuova

Le domande, in ordine: Where were you…? (dov’eri) → Who’s …? (chi è) → What does he look like? (com’è fatto) → What’s he like? (com’è di carattere).

Le risposte tipiche: I was at a concert with Amy. · He’s Amy’s new boyfriend. · He’s short and slim and he’s got blond, curly hair and green eyes. · He’s great! He’s quite lazy, but he’s very funny.

2. Parlare di una foto delle vacanze

  • Where were you in the photo? – I was in Devon, on holiday.
  • Who were you with? – With my cousin and her two friends.
  • When was it? – Two years ago.
  • Who was the photographer? – My aunt. / It was a selfie.
  • What was the weather like? – It was sunny every day.
  • What was the food like? – It was great.
  • Were you in a hotel? – No, we weren’t. We were in a campsite.
  • What was the best part of the holiday? – The beach!

Nel parlato was e were si pronunciano deboli: /wəz/ e /wə/. Nello scritto però si scrivono sempre per intero.

Reading: il Globe Theatrecomprensione del testo con domande aperte

Nella verifica c’è un testo con domande a risposta aperta: devi rispondere con frasi tue, in inglese. Leggi il testo con calma, poi rispondi. Sotto trovi un esempio sul Globe Theatre, l’argomento del libro.

Shakespeare's Globe Theatre: a day out in London

Shakespeare's Globe is a famous theatre in the centre of London, next to the River Thames. On an activity day there you can take part in a lesson about the theatre, try on clothes from the Tudor period (1485–1603), watch a play by William Shakespeare and meet the actors.

A little history. Today's Globe is the third Globe Theatre! The first one was built in 1599, and the writer William Shakespeare had a big part in its success. The first play at the original Globe was his tragedy Julius Caesar. In 1613 there was a terrible fire, and that was the end of the first theatre. In 1614 another Globe was built: it was open for thirty years. Today's theatre was built in 1997 and it's very similar to the original: it's almost circular and there's no roof, so it's very cold for visitors in winter! More than 1,500 people can watch a play there: 700 standing and the others sitting.

Who was William Shakespeare? He was a famous English writer. He was born in Stratford-upon-Avon in 1564 and he died in 1616. His wife's name was Anne Hathaway and they had three children. Hamlet, Macbeth and Romeo and Juliet are some of his famous plays. Shakespeare had brown hair and dark eyes. Was he tall? Was he slim? We don't know! From his work we know he was funny, intelligent and creative.

Fun fact: in Shakespeare's time there weren't any women actors. Young men or boys were the women in the plays!

Come si risponde a una domanda aperta

  1. Trova nella domanda la parola interrogativa e il verbo: When was…? Where was…? What colour was…?
  2. Cerca nel testo la frase con quell’informazione.
  3. Rispondi con una frase intera che riusa il verbo della domanda: When was the Tudor period? → It was from 1485 to 1603. Non basta «1485–1603».
  4. Sostituisci il nome con il pronome: Where was Shakespeare born? → He was born in Stratford-upon-Avon.

Scrivere: il blog della visita e il tuo postproduzione scritta con was / were / had

La produzione scritta della verifica è un breve testo su un’esperienza passata o su una persona. Non deve essere lungo: deve essere corretto e completo.

La scaletta di un post su una gita

  1. Quando e dove: I was at the Globe Theatre last Saturday.
  2. Con chi: I was with my cousin and my aunt.
  3. Com’era: It was a fantastic day. The tour was two hours long and the ticket was only £15.
  4. La guida / le persone: Our guide was friendly and funny.
  5. Il tempo: It was hot because the weather was sunny and warm.
  6. La parte migliore e una nota negativa: The best part was the meeting with the actors. Unfortunately there weren’t any photos: there were «no photography» signs everywhere!

Consiglio del libro: un testo con solo le informazioni essenziali è noioso. Aggiungi dettagli: perché eri lì, se era un’occasione speciale, se la visita è durata troppo o troppo poco.

La scaletta per descrivere una persona del passato

My grandad was born in 1950. When he was young he was tall and slim and he had black, curly hair. His eyes were green. He had a red Vespa. He wasn’t shy: he was very funny! Nota i tre verbi: was/were per com’era, had per cosa aveva, wasn’t per la negativa.

Gli errori che costano punti

  • I were, he were, you was, they was → controlla il soggetto.
  • she was black hair → i capelli si hanno: she had.
  • in the last week, yesterday night → last week, last night.
  • hairs → hair. i was → I was (sempre maiuscolo).

Tradurre e correggeredall’italiano all’inglese · caccia all’errore

Ultimo allenamento prima della verifica: tradurre frasi brevi e trovare gli errori tipici.

Gli errori da trovare

Quando correggi una frase, fatti tre domande: 1. Il soggetto è singolare o plurale (was o were)? 2. Presente o passato (is/has got o was/had)? 3. Com’era (be) o cosa aveva (have)?
